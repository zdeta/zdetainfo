3. Research Objective

The ZDC (Zdeta Coin) project is a digital research instrument designed to observe processes occurring in peer-to-peer networks. Our goal is to gather data on how users, algorithms, and blockchain protocols interact under conditions of limited resource availability. We study how a raw stimulus (contract availability) initiates interactions, creating connection structures between wallets without external manipulation of parameters (constant difficulty 215).

4. Anomaly Detection and Graph Architecture

The ZDC system monitors transactional relationships in real-time. Our algorithms track the "flow of influence" within the connection graph, focusing on detecting deviations from the organic model:

* System Entropy: We monitor rapid changes in the rate of interaction (phase transitions).
* Pattern Detection (Anomaly Detection): Algorithms flag transaction clusters exhibiting characteristics of automation (bot-driven) or artificial grouping.
* Propagation Visualization: We analyze how "knowledge" of the contract translates into the growth of active nodes.

5. Appeal to Observers

Every interaction with the ZDC contract is automatically recorded as a data point in our experiment. The user is not an investor – they are a node in a complex system whose behaviors are subject to statistical analysis.

"Complex systems reveal their true nature only under the influence of information stress. We are observing."

6. Research Potential: Extrapolation to Macro Systems

Observations gathered within the ZDC ecosystem are not an end in themselves. They constitute a "model dataset" that may serve in the future to analyze anomalies in other, more complex markets:

Pre-Bubble Detection: By analyzing the connection graph structure in ZDC, we are working on algorithms identifying "unnatural node densities" – patterns that often precede rapid liquidity changes in larger financial markets.

Information Propagation Efficiency Analysis: ZDC allows for the study of how quickly a "narrative" (information about the project) turns into "activity" (transactions). These same propagation parameters can be used to model the price behavior of high-volatility assets, where crowd psychology dominates over economic fundamentals.

System Resilience Modeling (Stress Testing): We use ZDC to test how systems react to Sybil attacks (creating multiple fake identities in a network). Understanding how to sift such anomalies from organic behavior is crucial for building the more secure transaction systems of the future.

Capital Flow Mapping in Chaotic Systems:Most financial models assume the "rationality" of market participants. Our observations of complex systems in ZDC allow for the creation of Agent-Based Modeling that better reflects the real, often irrational nature of capital markets.
